UsageNotch for Android 1.6.0 updates itself, adds a Classic style next to liquid glass, a tokens widget and a "your week" image to share.

**Updates itself**
- When a new version is published, the app downloads it from GitHub and installs it: in the background on Wi-Fi, or with **Update** on Overview and in Settings, with progress.
- Every download is checked before it installs: it must come from GitHub over HTTPS, match the SHA-256 listed in the release, be a newer version, and carry exactly the same signature as the app you have. Anything else is deleted.
- The first time, Android asks you to let UsageNotch install apps and to confirm. After that, Android 12 and later install updates without asking. Turn it off in Settings → Updates.

**Classic style**
- Settings → Appearance → **Style**: **Liquid glass** or **Classic**.
- Classic is the calm grouped look of iOS settings: solid cards on a grouped background, continuous corners, hairline separators, iOS-style segmented controls and buttons, and frosted floating bars that blur what scrolls beneath them (Android 12+). No refraction, no drifting light.
- It switches instantly and keeps your tab and scroll position. Widgets follow it with solid rounded bodies.

**Tokens widget**
- What Claude Code (or Codex) used today on your PC: the total, the API-price equivalent, the top project and a 7-day sparkline. From 2×1 up; long-press to choose the tool. Needs UsageNotch for Windows 2.6.

**Share your week**
- History → **Share your week** makes a story-sized image: your rings, each provider's week, your busiest day and streak, and your tokens and top project. It contains no account names and needs no storage permission.

**Haptics**
- A confirming tick when a refresh finishes, a firmer pattern when a limit crosses your warning (stronger at your urgent level) while you watch, a celebratory double tap when a limit renews, and light ticks while you scrub a chart. They follow Android's touch-feedback setting, and Reduce motion turns them off.
